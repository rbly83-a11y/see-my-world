## 1. Why these five journeys — and what the set does not cover

### 1.1 Selection justification

The five journeys were selected because, between them, they cover the complete trust spine of the accepted slice end to end — the sequence the README states as the slice's purpose: *a private World, a bounded authorized Lens grant, and a visitor Perspective* — with no step of that spine left unmapped and no journey depending on a capability the slice does not have.

| Lifecycle moment | Journey | Why it is primary |
|---|---|---|
| **Creation** | J-01 Owner creates a World and authors first content | Every other journey presupposes an empty, owner-controlled World with owner-declared content. This is slice steps 1–3 (create World → add Territory → declare topics) and PRD Steps A1 (as bounded by the slice: no First Light draft), A3, and CORE-COR-01 authoring. |
| **Correction** | J-02 Owner corrects in Studio | Correction is the only authoritative write path to a World (PRD Step A2) and the deepest-specified, most-tested behaviour in the slice (testimony-v1, revision-CAS, idempotency, Receipts). If correction is wrong, nothing downstream can be trusted. |
| **Sharing** | J-03 Owner shares | Sharing is where private state becomes a bounded disclosure decision: Lens creation, literal preview, named-recipient Invitation, and — after F-02 — atomic grant + derived projection creation (PRD Step A4). |
| **Viewing** | J-04 The named visitor views the Perspective | The visitor path is the trust model under test: recipient binding (F-01), request-time grant checks, resolution-bounded payloads, and identical generic denials for ordinary authorization failures (PRD Step A5). |
| **Revocation / expiry / quarantine / deletion** | J-05 Revocation, expiry, quarantine, and deletion | The lifecycle must close as firmly as it opens: revocation defeating a previously served view, expiry failing closed, replay quarantine surviving restart (F-03), and honest logical deletion (PRD Steps A3–A4; invariants 4, 16, 17, 37). |

Together the five journeys exercise every S02 acceptance test that describes a user-visible path (S02-01–S02-11, S02-13–S02-17, plus the F-01…F-04 remediation tests) and every requirement family the slice build report §3 lists as exercised: CORE-PW-*, CORE-COR-*, CORE-LENS-* (exercised portions), and CORE-VIS-*.

### 1.2 What this set does NOT cover — and why

The following are **excluded from these maps by design**, each for a corpus-stated reason. Their absence is a scope fact, not an oversight, and no step in J-01…J-05 smuggles any of them in:

- **First Light draft generation** (PRD Step A1, CORE-FL-01…08) — excluded from the accepted slice: the World starts empty and owner-controlled, with no draft generation (README step 1; build report §5; register OQ-4). J-01 therefore begins at deliberate owner declaration, not at a generated draft.
- **Seed saving** (PRD Step A6, CORE-SEED-01…07) — excluded from the slice (build report §§3, 5). No journey shows a visitor saving a Seed, and J-05 states the revocation truth without claiming any slice behaviour for held Seeds.
- **SEE YOURS** (PRD Step A7, CORE-SY-01…06) — excluded from the slice (build report §5). No journey shows a visitor beginning their own World.
- **Comparison** (and the Encounter Engine) — not part of the adopted minimum journey exercised by the slice; excluded from the pilot scope mapped here (PRD §11).
- **Anonymous visiting — Deferred (AUD-01).** Anonymous visitor access is **Deferred pending separate Founder reconciliation and authorization (G-17)**; D03-02 v1.1 classifies VST-07 (anonymous opening) as Deferred. Named-recipient binding is the **only accepted access model**: audience is a named-recipient Invitation only, never a public link (register OQ-8), and after F-01 the visitor must additionally authenticate with their own visitor credential. CORE-VIS-02 specifies anonymous opening *where the grant permits*; the slice's grants do not. J-04 therefore maps the named, credentialed visitor only, and maps credential-less access as a denial branch — not as a viewing path. This records the current accepted state **without deciding the future of anonymous access**.

Also outside this set, for the same reason (named in build report §5 as unbuilt by design): general Lens compilation over owner data and its test S02-12 (gated on Q-09); edges in any form (OQ-7; CORE-COR-10 excluded with edges); forwarding/resharing (Q-10); retention-period behaviour (Q-13).

## 2. J-01 — Owner creates a World and authors first content

**Actor:** Owner (authenticated owner principal).
**Goal:** Go from no World to a private, empty, owner-controlled World containing a first Territory grouping and first owner-declared topics, visible in the Explorer owner view.
**Preconditions:** The owner holds a valid owner credential (in the slice, the owner token resolved server-side to the owner principal; the principal is taken from session context, never from a request body — CORE-COR-05 order). No World need exist yet. No First Light draft is generated at any point in this journey.

**Requirements engaged:** CORE-PW-01, CORE-PW-02, CORE-PW-03, CORE-PW-04, CORE-PW-06 (ledger/projection mechanics), CORE-COR-01 (topic declaration entry weights), CORE-LENS-05 (by consequence: only testified material can ever be shared — see J-03/J-04).
**Acceptance tests engaged:** S02-01, S02-02 (creation/authoring path of the suite), S02-08 (reopen persistence, step 7), S02-10 (Explorer/Studio revision and digest agreement, step 6).

### Success path

| # | Screen | Actor action | System response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OWN-01 Owner sign-in / session entry | Owner presents their owner credential. | System resolves the credential server-side to the owner principal and establishes the owner session. All subsequent owner operations are authorised against this principal. |
| 2 | OWN-02 World creation | Owner creates a World. | System creates a World that is **empty and owner-controlled** (README step 1): private by default (CORE-PW-01), owned by exactly this owner, with no draft content, no imported material, and no First Light generation. The creation is recorded as an event in the append-only testimony ledger; current state is the deterministic projection (fold) of the ledger (CORE-PW-06). |
| 3 | OWN-03 World home / Explorer owner view | Owner opens their World in Explorer. | Explorer serves the **owner's own view** — the empty state: a valid World with no Territories and no topics yet. An empty World is a valid state, not an error (PRD Step A1/A3 empty-state rule, carried into the slice as the empty start). Explorer and Studio report the same revision and digest for the World (S02-10). |
| 4 | OWN-05 Territory grouping management | Owner adds a Territory grouping and names it. | System adds the Territory as a **provisional visual grouping inside the World** (README step 2). The interface states the provisional meaning: a Territory is not an Atlas entity and creates no Atlas geography. The addition is a ledger event; revision advances. |
| 5 | OWN-06 Studio topic declaration / authoring | Owner declares their first topic(s) in Studio, supplying the initial state the authoring operation requires. | System records each declaration as a `TOPIC_DECLARED` event with the normative entry weights (DECLARED 1.0, CONFIRMED 0.6, PROPOSED 0) and an owner-supplied initial strength in [0, 1] (build report §4.1). | 6 | OWN-03 World home / Explorer owner view | Owner returns to Explorer to inspect the result. | Explorer owner view shows the Territory grouping and the declared topic(s) as the owner's private working surface. Revision and digest agree with Studio (S02-10). |
| 7 | *(no screen — session boundary; see Coverage notes, §8)* | Owner leaves and later reopens the World in a fresh session/process. | After a fresh start, the World, its revision, and its digest are intact; replaying the ledger reproduces the same digest (README step 6; S02-08). |
| 7-D | **DEPENDENCY** — no screen | Returning-owner re-entry. | **DEPENDENCY:** The owner principal is resolved from a server-side token mapping (configuration). This is an unresolved dependency requiring a G2 decision; no re-entry flow is specified or invented here. |

### Failure / denial branches

- **J-01-F1 — Unauthenticated access denied.** At OWN-01, a request with no credential, or a credential the system cannot resolve to the owner principal, is denied. No World data, no existence information beyond the generic denial, and no partial session is returned. Every owner mutation follows the same order — ownership → validation → idempotency → expected-revision CAS → versioned rule → one atomic persist (build report §1, service description) — and fails closed at the ownership step.
- **J-01-F2 — Creating content in a World the principal does not own.** At OWN-05 or OWN-06, an authenticated principal attempts to add a Territory or declare a topic in a World owned by a different principal. The operation is denied at the ownership check, before validation or persistence; no ledger event is written, the target World's revision and digest are unchanged, and the denial reveals no private topic state (CORE-COR-05 error semantics: 403 must not reveal private topic state).

### Classification notes — J-01

- **Existing / implemented (operations):** World creation (empty, owner-controlled), Territory addition, topic declaration with the recorded entry weights, ledger persistence, fresh-process reopen with identical revision/digest, Explorer/Studio revision agreement (S02-08, S02-10).
- **Prototype-only design:** the OWN-01…OWN-06 screen presentations, including the empty-state presentation at OWN-03 and the provisional-Territory wording at OWN-05.
- **Specified only / not in this journey:** First Light draft generation and its correction pass (CORE-FL-*) — excluded, see §1.2. No step in J-01 generates, imports, or infers content.

## 3. J-02 — Owner corrects in Studio

**Actor:** Owner (authenticated, owning the World — J-01 preconditions carry over).
**Goal:** Change the state of topics in their own World using the testimony-v1 correction operators, with a Receipt for every committed correction, under revision-CAS concurrency and idempotency.
**Preconditions:** A World exists with at least one declared topic (J-01 completed). The owner acts in Studio, the correction/authoring surface only. Testimony-v1 is frozen: BOOST, REDUCE, REJECT, KEEP_WATCHING, with materialise-decay-then-reduce under tidal-v1 before the reducer applies (CORE-COR-01, CORE-COR-07).

**Requirements engaged:** CORE-COR-01, CORE-COR-02 (v1 portion only — BOOST→DECLARED as specified in v1; Q-01/D-34 **not** resolved), CORE-COR-03, CORE-COR-04, CORE-COR-05, CORE-COR-06, CORE-COR-07, CORE-COR-08.
**Acceptance tests engaged:** S02-06 (revision conflict), S02-07 (idempotency + Receipt), S02-09 (disputed operation), S02-13 (REFUTED guard + RESTORE), S02-10 (Studio/Explorer agreement, consequential).

### Success path

| # | Screen | Actor action | System response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OWN-03 World home / Explorer owner view | Owner selects a topic to correct and moves to Studio. | System shows the topic at its current materialised state (strength after tidal decay to the authoritative time, provenance, testimony, buoy state — the five v1 dimensions, CORE-PW-02; there is no confidence field). |
| 2 | OWN-06 Studio topic declaration / authoring → OWN-07 Studio correction surface | Owner opens the correction surface for the topic. | System presents exactly the four v1 operators — **BOOST, REDUCE, REJECT, KEEP_WATCHING** — with the current expected revision. No CONFIRM action is presented (see J-02-F4). REMOVE_BUOY and RESTORE are presented, where applicable, as their own distinct operations, not as correction types (CORE-COR-08; build report §4.2) — in D03-02 they carry their own screens: OWN-09 (Studio remove-buoy operation) and OWN-08 (Studio restore operation). |
| 3 | OWN-07 Studio correction surface | Owner applies a correction (e.g. BOOST) with an idempotency key and the expected revision. | System processes the correction in the Ch. 25 order (CORE-COR-05): authenticate principal → authorise ownership → validate topic and correction enum → check idempotency key → verify expected revision → apply the versioned testimony-v1 rule → persist state + event + Receipt + idempotency result in **one transaction**. BOOST applies `strength ← min(1, s × 1.5)`, `testimony += 1.0`, provenance upgrades to DECLARED, `last_witnessed` updates (CORE-COR-01). REDUCE, REJECT, and KEEP_WATCHING apply their own frozen arithmetic per CORE-COR-01; the three owner choices of CORE-COR-08 are kept distinct — KEEP_WATCHING never confirms, REMOVE_BUOY (its own operation) neither rejects nor erases history, REJECT excludes and is reversible only through RESTORE. |
| 4 | OWN-10 Correction Receipt view | Owner inspects the result. | System returns the committed result with a **Receipt** naming the algorithm version (`testimony-v1`) and the rule applied (CORE-COR-06; S02-07). Receipts are queries against the Testimony Ledger, not generated explanations. Studio and Explorer now report the same new revision and digest (S02-10). |

### Failure / denial branches

- **J-02-F1 — Revision conflict (stale revision → 409, SYS-05).** The owner submits a correction against a revision that is no longer current (a second, conflicting revision has committed first). System responds **409** and presents **SYS-05 Revision conflict**: nothing is applied, no ledger event is written, and the owner is directed to retry deliberately against the updated World — never a silent overwrite (CORE-COR-05; PRD Step A2 recovery rule; S02-06).
- **J-02-F2 — Idempotent retry returns the original result.** The owner (or their client) retries a correction with the **same idempotency key and an identical body** — e.g. after a network failure where commit status was unknown. System returns the **original committed result** and applies no change twice (invariant 5; S02-07). Related collision: the same key with a **different body** returns **409** (S02-07) and applies nothing.
- **J-02-F3 — REFUTED guard and RESTORE.** The owner attempts an ordinary correction (any of the four operators) on a topic whose provenance is REFUTED. System fails the correction with **"Explicit restoration required"** (CORE-COR-03; S02-13); strength and provenance are unchanged. Recovery is a separate path, not a branch of the correction: at OWN-08 (Studio restore operation), the owner invokes **RESTORE as its own operation** — requiring owner authorisation, explicit confirmation, a current revision check, and an idempotency key — producing a `TOPIC_RESTORED` event in which testimony persists as history and status/strength reset to an owner-selected state, never automatically to the pre-refutation strength. Only after RESTORE commits can ordinary correction proceed (S02-13).
- **J-02-F4 — The disputed operation: CONFIRM submitted as a correction type is rejected.** The owner (or a client built against a different assumption) submits `CONFIRM` — or likewise `RESTORE` — as a correction *type*. System rejects it (**400**); the exposed action set is exactly the four v1 operators (S02-09). The interface must present this honestly: **there is no CONFIRM operation in v1**. Whether confirmation should be an explicit CONFIRM event distinct from BOOST is the open conflict Q-01/D-34; this journey preserves that conflict and resolves nothing (CORE-COR-02).
- **J-02-F5 — BOOST-from-zero: known defect behaviour, shown honestly.** The owner applies BOOST to a topic at strength 0. Under testimony-v1 as specified, `0 × 1.5 = 0`: **strength remains 0** and the topic is not BOOST-recovered (CORE-COR-04; S02 suite carries the defect in the reducer). The Receipt still records the correction and its testimony. The interface must not disguise this as success-with-effect, must not clamp zero to a positive value, and must not substitute the v2-experimental formula — the designated remedy is **not adopted**. This is specified v1 behaviour, presented as a known defect, not a malfunction of the moment.

### Classification notes — J-02

- **Existing / implemented (operations):** all four operators with frozen arithmetic, revision-CAS (409 on conflict), idempotency (original-result return; same-key/different-body 409), Receipt naming `testimony-v1` and the rule, REFUTED guard, RESTORE as a separate operation, CONFIRM/RESTORE-as-correction-type rejection, BOOST-from-zero defect carried (S02-06, S02-07, S02-09, S02-13).
- **Prototype-only design:** OWN-07 correction surface layout, OWN-10 Receipt presentation, SYS-05 conflict presentation.
- **Specified only:** the complete provenance transition table (CORE-COR-09) remains blocked on Q-06 and is not completed by this journey; edge corrections (CORE-COR-10) are excluded with edges (OQ-7).

## 4. J-03 — Owner shares

**Actor:** Owner (authenticated, owning the World).
**Goal:** Share a bounded view of their World with one named recipient: provision the recipient's visitor principal, create a Lens, preview exactly what the visitor will receive, and issue the Invitation such that the grant and its derived projection exist together or not at all.
**Preconditions:** A World with testified content exists (J-01, J-02). The recipient is a specific, named person — audience is a named-recipient Invitation only, never a public link (OQ-8). In the slice, the visitor's Perspective is served from fixed, synthetic, preapproved projection fixtures; general Lens compilation over owner data does not take place (gated on Q-09, S02-12 not implemented). The preview step below is specified against CORE-LENS-02 and, in the slice, holds in the form recorded in build report §3: *preview = the served payload itself* (CORE-LENS-02, exercised portion).

**Requirements engaged:** CORE-LENS-01, CORE-LENS-02, CORE-LENS-03, CORE-LENS-05, CORE-LENS-07 (expiry set at creation), CORE-LENS-09 (grants/expiry portion only; forwarding portion blocked on Q-10 and not specified here), CORE-PW-05 (the Worldprint itself is never shared — only a Lens-derived projection).
**Acceptance tests engaged:** S02-03 (served payload deep-equal to the fixture, field by field), F-02 remediation test (atomic grant + projection), S02-14 (expiry set here, enforced in J-05).

### Success path

| # | Screen | Actor action | System response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OWN-14 Visitor-principal provisioning | Owner provisions the named recipient: registers the recipient as a visitor principal. | System issues a per-recipient visitor credential (shown once, stored only as a SHA-256 hash, distinct from any invitation token) bound server-side to that recipient identity (F-01 fix, build report §7). Token possession alone will never be sufficient for this recipient — see J-04. The credential is presented to the owner and recipient as what it is: a **prototype identity mechanism** binding a request to the named recipient within the prototype — never as identity verification or proof of legal identity (D03-04 row 14; B-01). Provisioning is owner-authenticated. |

The A-48 qualification applies: the prototype registry does not establish secure credential delivery. This is an unresolved dependency requiring a G2 decision; no delivery mechanism is specified or invented here. |
| 2 | OWN-11 Lens creation | Owner creates a Lens: selects the **territory set**, the **resolution** (Continent / Coast / Harbour), and the **expiry**. Audience is the named recipient from step 1. | System records the Lens as the tuple *(territory set, resolution, audience, expiry)* (CORE-LENS-01). Publication requires this explicit owner action; silence, inactivity, and prior publication are not consent. **Split classification (D03-02 v1.1):** the grant-creation operation (step 4) is an **Existing operation**; selecting a territory set here is a **Prototype-only control with no backend effect** in the accepted implementation — `createGrant()` takes no territory-set parameter — and must not be portrayed as operational. |
| 3 | OWN-12 Lens preview + Perspective Passport | Owner previews the Lens before issuing anything. | System shows the **exact visible result the visitor will receive, rendered from the compiled payload itself** — in the slice, the preview *is* the served payload (CORE-LENS-02). No grant enumeration or public-link form exists. The grant is bound to audience and expiry and is revocable (CORE-LENS-01, CORE-LENS-09 grants portion). |

This is an unresolved dependency requiring a G2 decision; no delivery mechanism is specified or invented here. |
| 5 | OWN-13 Invitation / grant management | Owner confirms the issued state. | System shows the active grant (recipient, resolution, expiry, revocation control) as the standing disclosure fact for this World. Later changes to the World do not silently alter the published Perspective; republication is a new owner action (PRD Step A4, returning-user rule). |

### Failure / denial branches

- **J-03-F1 — Preview shows material that must never appear (CORE-LENS-05).** During step 3, if the preview payload were to contain any PROPOSED or REFUTED material — in any form, including as an anonymised or generalised trace — publication must not proceed: in the pilot, no Perspective contains a PROPOSED or WITNESSED node at any resolution under any Lens (invariant 20), and a REFUTED node's internal record is never disclosed through a Perspective (invariant 13). In the slice this holds structurally: the synthetic fixtures contain only compiled public fields, and the PROPOSED and REFUTED fixture topics are absent from every payload (build report §1, fixtures). The owner-facing rule stands regardless of mechanism: **a preview containing such material is a stop, not a warning to click through.**
- **J-03-F2 — Grant creation failure leaves neither grant nor projection (F-02).** During step 4, if projection creation fails (in the remediation test, a deterministic fault injected between the two writes, inside the transaction), `createGrant()` throws and the transaction rolls back: **afterwards neither the grant nor the projection exists.** The owner sees a failure at OWN-13 with no partial grant listed; with the fault cleared, the same issuance call persists both together. There is no state in which a live grant points at a missing projection, or an orphan projection exists without its grant.

### Classification notes — J-03

- **Existing / implemented (operations):** visitor-principal provisioning with hashed, per-recipient credentials (F-01); grant creation with token shown once and stored hashed; atomic grant + projection creation (F-02 test); expiry recorded on the grant; served payload identical to the fixture payload (S02-03). **Territory-set split:** grant creation is an Existing operation; the territory-set selection control at OWN-11 is **Prototype-only with no backend effect** — `createGrant()` takes no territory-set parameter — and is not operational in the accepted implementation.
- **Prototype-only design:** OWN-11 Lens creation screens, OWN-12 preview + Passport presentation, OWN-13 grant management, OWN-14 provisioning screens.
- **Specified only:** the eight-step Lens Compiler sequence over owner data (CORE-LENS-04), disclosure eligibility generalisation (CORE-LENS-06), and the field-level schema per resolution (CORE-LENS-08) — the compiler contract is specified but not executed over owner data; fixtures stand in its place (build report §3, R-01). **Q-09 is not resolved by this journey**, and no step above claims field-level disclosure behaviour beyond the fixture payloads and the CORE-LENS-05 exclusion rule.

## 5. J-04 — The named visitor views the Perspective

**Actor:** Visitor — the specific named recipient of the Invitation issued in J-03, holding (a) the invitation token and (b) their own visitor credential.
**Goal:** Open the Perspective at exactly the granted resolution, walk its finite set of discovery objects, and ask "Why is this here?" of what they are shown.
**Preconditions:** A live grant exists (J-03 completed): not revoked, not expired, projections not quarantined (J-05 states the contrary cases). The visitor's credential was provisioned to them as the named recipient (J-03 step 1). Access checks occur at request time; a manifest is not an authorisation token (invariant 42).

**Requirements engaged:** CORE-VIS-01, CORE-VIS-03, CORE-VIS-04, CORE-VIS-05, CORE-VIS-06, CORE-LENS-03, CORE-LENS-05, CORE-LENS-07 (request-time enforcement), CORE-LENS-09 (Invitation as recipient grant).
**Acceptance tests engaged:** S02-03 (payload deep-equal to fixture; no `strength`/`testimony` keys on the wire), S02-04 (escalation, cross-World token, visitor-token-as-owner, forged token — identical generic denials for ordinary authorization failures), F-01 remediation tests (wrong recipient with valid token; binding after fresh restart).

And absence carries no stance: a topic or label missing from the Perspective is missing because it was not included, not testified, not eligible, or was generalised/omitted under the grant — it must never be presented or read as disagreement, rejection, or dislike (D03-04 B-11).

### Denial branches — ordinary authorization failures, all producing the identical generic denial (SYS-01)

Every branch below is an **ordinary authorization failure** and terminates at **SYS-01 Generic denial**: the same status (generic 403) and the same generic body, byte-identical across causes, with no existence or reason leakage — in the F-01 test, denial bodies are byte-identical to the forged-token denial. No branch returns a partial view (CORE-VIS-02 rule, applied to the slice's credentialed model). **Carve-out:** quarantine is **not** one of these branches and is **not** a generic denial — it is a distinct response class (503 `PERSPECTIVE_UNAVAILABLE`, SYS-04), checked **before** all authorization checks in `getVisitorPerspective()`, and handled in J-05 Path C. The two classes must never be conflated.

- **J-04-D1 — Wrong recipient holding a valid token (F-01).** A different provisioned visitor presents the valid invitation token with **their own** (wrong-recipient) credential. Recipient binding fails: the resolved recipient ≠ the grant's named recipient. → SYS-01. *(This branch is the A-47 acceptance blocker, closed by the F-01 fix and its tests, including after a fresh restart.)*
- **J-04-D2 — Forged credential.** The valid token is presented with a credential that resolves to no visitor principal. → SYS-01.
- **J-04-D3 — Missing credential.** The valid token is presented with no visitor credential at all. Token possession alone is not enough. → SYS-01.
- **J-04-D4 — Spoofed recipient claim.** The request asserts a recipient identity (e.g. a `?recipientId=` claim) matching the grant, without the matching credential. The submitted claim is never read; binding is resolved only from the credential, server-side. → SYS-01.
- **J-04-D5 — Cross-World token.** A valid token issued for a different World is presented against this World. The grant/World check fails. → SYS-01 (S02-04).
- **J-04-D6 — Resolution escalation attempt.** The visitor requests a finer resolution than the grant allows (e.g. Harbour detail under a Coast grant). Detail(Output) ≤ Resolution(Grant) holds structurally — finer data is absent from the payload, not suppressed in display — and the escalation attempt fails closed. → SYS-01 (S02-04; invariant 9).
- **J-04-D7 — Visitor token presented as owner.** An invitation token (or visitor credential) is presented on an owner route as if it were the owner principal. The owner side resolves principals only from its own server-side mapping. → SYS-01 (S02-04).

## 6. J-05 — Revocation, expiry, quarantine, and deletion

**Actor:** Owner (revocation, deletion) and Visitor (the party whose access ends); quarantine is a system state the owner inspects.
**Goal:** Close the lifecycle: end a grant by revocation or expiry with fail-closed effect, survive a replay-integrity failure with projections quarantined, and delete a World with honest wording about what deletion means.
**Preconditions:** For revocation/expiry branches: a live grant exists (J-03) and the visitor has previously been served the Perspective (J-04). For quarantine: a replay verification mismatch has occurred. For deletion: the owner owns the World.

**Requirements engaged:** CORE-LENS-07, CORE-PW-06 (replay; invariant 17), CORE-PW-08 (mechanics portion; periods portion blocked on Q-13 and not specified here), CORE-VIS-02 (no partial view on failure), CORE-VIS-05 (caches hold only compiled payloads, invalidated on revocation/expiry).
**Acceptance tests engaged:** S02-05 (revocation defeats a previously served view; `no-store` asserted), S02-14 (expired grant fails closed), S02-11 (replay mismatch → quarantine → visitor path serves nothing), F-03 remediation test (quarantine persists across restart), S02-15, S02-16, S02-17 (logical deletion and crash/recovery behaviour at the storage boundary).

### Path E — The standing truth about revocation (presented in Paths A and at issuance in J-03)

The product states plainly, at grant issuance (OWN-13) and again at revocation:

> **Revocation cannot erase what a visitor already saw or copied while the grant was valid.** Revoking ends future access, blocks new opens and in-progress actions under the grant, and invalidates projections and caches. It does not reach copies, notes, screenshots, or memory held outside the system. (CORE-LENS-07; Bible Ch. 17 §17.4.)

This wording is a requirement of the journey, not a disclaimer appended to it: any presentation of revocation that omits it overstates the control the owner actually has.
